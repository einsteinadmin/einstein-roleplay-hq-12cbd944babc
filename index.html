What we're testing right now

The ask is narrow: does the call work? Run it from ring-ring through to asking permission to build the estimate, and tell us where it feels wrong.

Grading stops at the permission ask. Intake, pricing, the estimate and the close are not scored — those are later phases. Nothing past "would it make sense to build a quote now?" counts.

Talk, don't type. v1.4 added a 🎤 button — it's a phone call, and practicing out loud is the point. Typing works too if you'd rather.

The method it trains

Set their agenda, not ours. The opener, memorized verbatim:

"Let's talk about your move — what are your biggest concerns, and what are the most important things you want answers to on this call today?"

Then the concern loop, two or three times: draw out a concern → probe it a layer or two → "let me put your mind at ease" + the one differentiator that answers it → "any other concerns?" → then "anything else?" → then ask permission.

Ruled 8/21 — concerns are answered in the moment, not banked. Jeff's playbook deck currently places the differentiators after the price, which would score zero for answering inside the loop. Jeff re-confirmed the loop verbally and it held up in the live role-play, so this is version lag in the deck, not a change of method. The app scores the loop.

Roadmap

The app trains one phase today. This is the whole arc.

1
Set the context — ring-ring through the permission ask. Live now. Next inside this phase: hooks, live coaching, and a post-call board showing what got left on the table.
2
Intake — the eight fields clean and fast, plus the hidden-scope asks.
3
Get the estimate right — hours and crew size. The existing curriculum already has answer keys, so this phase grades itself.
4
The close — the booking ask, "I need to think it over," money answered straight.
5
The full call — all of it, end to end.
6
Traps — what we don't do, so nobody promises it. Likely a quiz rather than a role-play.
7
Customer service — live-move complaints and questions.

Open — needs a decision

The call-grading rubric. Jeff's playbook proposes a 100-point instrument (C·O·N·C·E·R·N) replacing today's 85-point rubric, with 60 points scored before any number is said. The interim agreed in the room: the 85-point rubric goes on ice, and the weekly measure becomes did you engage with the homework — progressive skill building, one element at a time, rather than a percentage score.

The knock-on: Amanda's Q3 goal for close-rate improvement had its bars written against the 85-point instrument, so those bars need revisiting now that we're not scoring against it. Amanda owns that call, with Jeff's input.
Live coaching — how much help is too much? If the app tells a rep what to say, they're practicing transcription rather than selling. If it never helps, reps who feel bad will abandon the method. Proposal is a withdrawal schedule: free help early, counted in the middle, none at the end. Unruled.
Promised notes have to land somewhere. Part of the method is offering to make a special note for the crew — "I'll flag your grandmother's dresser so the crew lead knows before they walk in." Move alerts are real and crew leads read them, but we need a way to confirm reps actually write them. Teaching a promise the system doesn't keep is worse than not teaching it.
